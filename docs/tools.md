# The tools

[< Docs index](README.md)

The Home page groups the tools as cards; the sidebar mirrors the same groups. A **Dark mode** switch sits at the bottom of
the sidebar (first launch follows Windows; your choice is remembered). Home shows every tool on one screen: soft bands, each holding one to three groups divided by a hairline, with the tool cards inside.

## On every page

- **Adding comics.** Each page has the same box at the top: drop comics or a folder on it, or use **Add folder…** or **Choose comics…**
  inside it. On the folder tools (Bulk folder, Folder icons, Renamer, Metadata, CBR to CBZ, Library audit) *Choose comics…* works on
  just the files you pick, not the rest of their folder; choosing a folder again goes back to the whole folder. (Library audit then judges
  duplicates and gaps among the picked comics only; Folder icons uses one folder per parent, with the first or last picked comic as its cover.)
  Reading order and ACEO sheets add whatever you give them to their list. **Single issue** takes one comic; its folder button opens the folder in Bulk folder.
- **The sidebar and Home sections.** The tool list in the sidebar scrolls when the window is short; *Home* and *Library stats* stay at the top, and the *Reorder sections…* button and the dark-mode switch stay pinned at the bottom. Click a section heading (▾ / ▸) to fold or unfold its tools. **Reorder sections…** opens a small window to move the headings up and down (or Reset); the sidebar and the Home bands follow immediately, and Home repacks the groups into rows so every tool still fits on one screen. The order and which sections are folded are remembered.
- **Resizing.** Drag the thin divider to resize the sidebar, an options panel (on the pages that still have one), the right column of Bulk folder, Metadata and Library audit, the Renamer's file list (beside the card) and the ACEO cover list (beside the
  preview). Drag the edge of any table heading to resize that column, and use the scrollbar under a table when its columns are wider than the window.
  The sidebar and panel widths are remembered; column widths are not.
- **Two kinds of page, during the redesign.** *Bulk folder*, *Metadata* and *Library audit* are built on the new layout: a header, **two columns with a divider you drag** (what you choose on the left, what you can set and the review on the right, which scrolls on its own; the width is remembered and the left column never gets narrower than it can use) and a slim footer holding the main button and a line of status. *Library stats* is built on the same header and footer but as a stack of sections that fold to a one-line summary. The other pages still have an **options panel** that reads in the same order everywhere: *Source* (what to read), the tool's own groups, then *Output* (where results go and what happens to originals), with the main button at the bottom of the panel and utility buttons (Rescan, Open folder) below a divider. Each page moves to the new layout in turn; see the [roadmap](roadmap.md).
- **Buttons are grouped** by what they do, with a small divider between groups (for example Reading order: *Suggest order* | *Top ▲ ▼ Bottom* | *Remove Clear*).

## Library stats

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Library stats** | Scan your library once and see it at a glance. Open it from the sidebar, or from the summary line at the top of Home (`4,210 comics · 86 series · 48.2 GB · scanned 2 days ago`, with a large **Open Stats** button and a small refresh icon (↻) beside it; before anything has been scanned the line offers *Choose your library folder and scan it*). Nothing is changed. |

The page has three segments (the shared layout; a finished segment folds to a one-line summary and the next opens by itself).

**Library.** Choose the library folder by dropping it on the page, with **Change…** in the strip, or from **Recent ▾** (it is remembered and scanned straight away). A shut *Advanced options* block holds *Include subfolders* and **Read page counts** (slower, adds total pages). The footer's **Rescan library** repeats the scan, which is quick for an unchanged library because of the cache; while scanning it reads **Stop**. Only `.cbz` and `.cbr` are counted, and `Archive` folders are skipped.

**Overview.** Tiles (comics, series, publishers, size on disk, average size, pages), then bars in three groups. *Collection*: **comics per series**, **per publisher**, **per year** (in time order) and **file formats**. *Health*: how complete the **metadata** is (ComicInfo.xml, year, publisher, issue number; single issues versus collected editions); a row with something missing has a **Fix these** link that opens the Metadata tool on the library. *Space*: the **largest files**. Every bar uses the one accent colour. Click a bar to list the comics behind it in a drawer at the right edge of the page (filterable, with **Open folder** and **Close**). *Chart options* (shut) holds **Split series into volumes**, **Group years into decades** and **Show every bar** (otherwise the top 15 and an *Other* bar). Missing values are shown as *Unknown*. The footer's **Export CSV…** offers every section; **Copy summary** puts a short text summary on the clipboard.

**Reading orders.** Folders of reading-order copies would be counted twice, so you mark them once and every tool leaves them out. The **Reading orders** section (shut by default) lists the marked folders with their comic counts and sizes, with **Mark a folder…** and **Unmark selected**. After a scan, a folder where most comics start with a number (`01 - Name`, `[02] Name`) or have a story arc is *suggested* on a Needs attention card: **Mark them** or **Not these** (a no is remembered). Marked folders are left out of every number on the page and of the Home line; the switch **Include reading orders in stats** (top of Overview, off by default) brings them back, and says how many comics are left out or included. The marked folders are saved with the settings.

**Needs attention.** Each card has a bold title with the count, a plain description of what the problem means and what its button does, and (for missing metadata) a few example file names. The cards: folders that look like reading orders, comics with no publisher, no year or no issue number (**Fix metadata**), and groups of possible duplicates with the same series, volume and issue (**Open Library audit**). The Home line shows how many comics need attention.

Two columns with a divider you drag. **Left**: **Comics** (a slim drop box, *Include subfolders*, *Include PDF and EPUB*) and **Review**, a list of cards, one per file: a tick box, the current name, the new name and where it goes, a status, and a **type chip** with a green, amber or red dot for how sure the reading of the name is. ## Metadata

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Metadata** | Repair the details stored inside your comics (`ComicInfo.xml`, the file YACReader and most readers use) by hand, with suggestions from the rest of your library. Nothing is written until you save. |

The page is two columns side by side. **Left, the comics**: drop box with *Library folder* and *Recent*, *Include subfolders*, a **Show** menu (All comics, Needs attention, No series, No issue number, No year, No publisher, No ComicInfo.xml) and a tall, filterable table with a mark for each comic (`✓` complete, `!` needs attention, `·` locked), its series, issue, volume, year and publisher (a dash where one is missing) and whether it has been edited. A `.cbr` is listed but locked, because a RAR cannot be written to; a note offers **Convert them…** (the CBR to CBZ tool, opened on the same folder). **Select all shown** picks every comic in the table. **Right, what you can edit** (it scrolls on its own, its width is the divider you drag, and it follows the selection at once): a switch chooses *Automatic* (one comic selected gives the one-comic form, several give the many-comics form), *One at a time* or *Several at once*. *One at a time* shows the cover, the comic's place in the list with **Previous** and **Next**, and every field (series, issue number, volume, year, title, publisher, issue count; series group, genre, alternate series and story arc under *Advanced options*); a field you have changed says "edited" beside its name. Under each field are **suggestion buttons** taken only from your own library: values used in the same series, in the same folder, read from the file name, or used elsewhere (hover a button to see which). One click fills the field. Setting a publisher, series group or genre offers to apply it to the other comics in the same series that have none. *Several at once* edits only the fields you fill in (an empty field keeps what each comic has): **Set for N comics**, **Fill blanks from file names**, and under *Advanced options* **Remove issue number** (YACReader sorts by issue number before file name, so removing it makes it use the file name) and **Keep it again**. **Scroll down in the right column to the changes**: every pending change as comic, field, what it was and what it becomes, kept current as you edit. A note says how many comics change, how many values are deleted and what happens to the originals (*Advanced options* chooses a backup in an `Archive` folder or replacing the file), and **Confirm and save N changes** is the only thing that writes. The slim footer holds **Review N changes** (it scrolls to the list and writes nothing) with **Discard edits** beside it. Other fields already in the XML are kept. The links on Library stats (**Fix these**, **Fix metadata**) open this page already filtered, with a chip saying where you came from and a **Back** button; saving re-scans Library stats.

## Library Audit

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Library audit** | Scans a library folder once and lists everything that needs attention in one table, with a strip of counts and a Details panel that points to the tool that fixes each kind of problem. Nothing changes until you tick findings and press **Fix N checked**. It replaces the old Library audit and Clean-up pages. |

**The page.** Two columns with a divider you can drag. **Left**: *Library* (drop a folder or comics on the box, *Library folder* and *Recent* are beside it, *Include subfolders*), which folds to a one-line summary after the first scan of a folder (click its title to open it again; a repeat scan does not fold it again), and the *Findings* below it, which take the rest of the height. **Right, always in view: Checks**: a plain switch for each kind of finding (Broken files, Duplicates, Missing issues, Junk files, CBR files, Warnings; they decide what is listed, counted and fixed, and take effect at once without a rescan), **Include reading orders in duplicates** (with a note saying how many comics are left out), and a shut *Advanced options* block whose title counts the settings you changed: **Full integrity test** (slow, reads every file completely), **Open real .cbr files** (slow, because a RAR is extracted whole), **Compare cover images** with Strict / Normal / Loose, **Expect issues from #1**, and the limits for pages, file size and cover height under *Warn about*. `Archive` folders are skipped. A repeat scan of an unchanged library is fast, because results are cached (see [Architecture](architecture.md#the-library-scan)). Pages, covers and junk files are always read.

**The list.** It opens with a **summary strip** ("1 broken file · 12 groups of duplicates · 4 series with gaps · 9 comics with junk · 38 CBR files"); click any count to open that list. **Details** at the right opens the details panel (below). The strip and the chips wrap onto more lines when the column is narrow. Chips under it filter the table and show a count each: **All** (every type except Warnings and Reading orders), **Broken**, **Duplicates** (counted in groups), **Missing** (counted in series), **Junk**, **CBR**, **Warnings** and **Reading orders** (counted in comics; shown when you have marked folders). A chip whose check is switched off is hidden. Click the box at the left of a row to tick it; **Tick all / Untick all** work on the rows you can see, **Ignore selected** and **Open folder** act on the selected rows, and the table has its own filter box and loads long lists as you scroll.

- **Broken.** Without the integrity test: files the scan notices (not a valid zip, no images, a cover that won't open). With it: the zip CRC test and the cover, middle and last pages must decode; real RAR files are checked with the extractor's own test command. Fix: move to `Archive` as `.bak`.
- **Duplicates.** Groups of probable copies, strongest evidence first: same series, volume and issue number (a single issue is never a copy of an Annual with the same number); identical file size; and, if **Compare cover images** is on, covers that look alike. A single issue and a collected edition are never grouped together, so a TPB is not a duplicate of its issues. Two copies of the same TPB (same series, volume and format) are. **Comics inside a folder marked as a reading order are left out** (they are deliberate copies; see below). The **best copy** is kept: an original outside a reading-order folder first, then most pages, then the taller cover, then the larger file; it has no tick box. Fix: tick the others to move them to `Archive` as `.bak`.
- **Missing.** One row per series with a line per type, such as `Issues (v2): #13, #27-#29 · TPB vols: 3, 5`. Issues are checked from the lowest number you own (or #1 with *Expect issues from #1*) up to the highest you own or the issue count in `ComicInfo.xml`. Collected editions are tracked separately for each format (TPB, Hardcover, Omnibus, …) by volume number. Annuals, specials, one-shots, decimals, ranges (`#1-12`) and files with no number are not tracked. **Copy wishlist** and **Save wishlist** (`.txt` or `.csv`) appear on this chip: `Batman v2 #13`, `Hellboy TPB vol 3`.
- **Junk.** Zip-based comics holding files other than pages and `ComicInfo.xml` (Thumbs.db, `.nfo`, `__MACOSX`, …). Pre-ticked. Fix: rewrite the archive without them (verified before it replaces the original).
- **CBR.** Every `.cbr`. Pre-ticked. Fix: convert to a verified `.cbz`, remove its junk, then delete the `.cbr`.
- **Warnings.** Fewer than 10 or more than 300 pages, under 1 MB or over 500 MB, a cover under 800 px tall (change the limits under *Warn about* in Checks; the list updates without a rescan). Hidden from **All**; nothing to fix automatically.
- **Reading orders.** One row per folder you marked as a reading order in Library stats: how many comics it holds and how many of them also exist in your library outside it. Nothing to fix. These comics are left out of Duplicates unless **Include reading orders in duplicates** is on (then a rescan is needed; a group with a copy inside and a copy outside always keeps the outside one). Mark or unmark folders in Library stats; the list is shared with every tool.

**Details.** The panel opens over the right half of the page, down to the footer, and closes with **Close** or by choosing a button in it. *This scan*: how many comics were scanned and how fast, which checks are on, whether the integrity test ran, how many real `.cbr` files were not opened, how many comics were left out as reading orders, and how many findings you ignored. *What was found, and what to do*: one block per kind with the count, a plain sentence and buttons that point to where it gets fixed: **Show in list** (the fix is here), **Convert in bulk** (opens CBR to CBZ on this folder), **Mark reading orders** and **View stats** (Library stats), and, when some comics have no series or no issue number, **Comics with no series** / **Comics with no issue number** (open the Metadata tool on this folder, already filtered, with a chip "From Library audit" and a Back button). The CBR list also has a **Convert in bulk** button, and the Missing list the wishlist buttons.

**Fixing.** The footer's main button reads **Fix N checked** (N counts the ticked rows of the list you are looking at). It first shows what will happen in a few lines, with a **Details** button that lists every file, what happens to it and whether that can be undone (a move to Archive can, a deleted `.cbr` or removed junk cannot); **Fix N files** goes ahead and **Cancel** changes nothing. The footer also holds **Rescan**, **Ignored…** and **Archive…**. A file with several fixes gets one: Archive beats convert, convert already cleans. Failures are shown in the row and the status, the original is left alone, and the list rescans afterwards (quick, thanks to the cache).

**Ignore.** Select rows and press **Ignore selected** to hide them from every scan. **Ignored…** lists what you've hidden and restores it. The list is remembered between sessions. Ignoring a Missing row hides the numbers it shows; numbers that go missing later still appear.

**Archive…** (in the footer) shows every file in the `Archive` folders under the library, with sizes. Tick files and **Purge ticked…** deletes them permanently (after a confirmation; they do not go to the Recycle Bin).

## Reading Orders

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Reading order** | Arrange issues (from one or several folders) in the order you want to read them and record it in each CBZ's `ComicInfo.xml` as a story arc, an alternate series, or both. The page is two columns with a divider you drag. **Left**: a slim drop box whose buttons share one row (*Add folder…*, *Choose comics…*, *Library folder*, *Recent* and **Open a reading order ▾**, a menu of the folders you marked as reading orders, which adds their comics; the row wraps when the column is narrow) and the **Order** list, one **card** for each comic (its number, name, what it will become, the arc it is in now and a status, with a tick box): hold a card and drag it anywhere to move it. The footer says **Stamp N comics**, with **Preview changes** and **Mark as reading order** beside it. When copies were made in a new folder the page asks once whether to **mark that folder as a reading order** (so Library stats and the duplicate check leave it out): *Mark it*, *Always mark new ones* or *Not now*; the Advanced setting (*Ask me*, *Always mark it*, *Never*) changes the habit. **Mark as reading order** marks the folder of the last copies, or the one folder that holds every listed issue. See [Reading Order](reading-order.md). |

## Add to Library

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Add to library** | Bring new comics in, checked, cleaned, named and filed. Two columns with a divider you drag. **Left**: **Inbox** (a slim drop box with *Add folder…*, *Choose comics…* and *Scan inbox*, the inbox folder, *Include subfolders* and **Watch this folder**) and **Review**, one card per comic with what it becomes (`Series\Name.cbz`, and the steps it needs: convert, clean, rename) and a status (*Ready*, *Duplicate*, *Exists*, *No series*, *Failed*). Comics that need a look are listed first with a red stripe and a one-line fix: **Set series…** (types the series for the selected comics; it changes the name, the series folder and the `Series` in the new comic's `ComicInfo.xml`), **Add anyway** (for a copy already in the library) and **Skip**. Click a card to see the full path it goes to. **Watch this folder** (off by default, remembered) looks at the inbox every few seconds while the app is open and lists a new comic once its size has stopped changing; it never adds anything by itself. **Right, scrolling on its own**: **Destination** (the Ready folder, *File into series folders*, *Name like the Renamer*, a line saying how names and folders are made, and a **Naming settings** button that opens the Renamer) and **Clean-up** (*Remove junk files*, *Rename pages 001.jpg…*, **The original comic**: delete it after the checks, move it to an Archive folder, or keep it, each with its consequence; *Advanced options*: extra junk patterns). Footer: **Add N comics** (it asks first; **Stop** while it runs, with the time left), **Open Ready folder** and, after a failure, **Retry N failed**. Only `.cbz` and `.cbr` are taken. |

The table shows each comic, what it **Becomes** (path inside the Ready folder), its **Steps** (convert, clean, rename) and a **Status**: *Ready* (ticked), or flagged and unticked: *Duplicate* (a comic with the same series, volume and issue is already in the Ready folder, or earlier in the same batch), *Exists* (that exact file name is taken) and *No series* (nothing to name it from, so it would keep its own name). Tick a flagged row to add it anyway; a taken name gets a free variant, so nothing is overwritten. *In place* means the comic already is where it belongs. Click a row to read the reason under the table.
